## The system

Incompressible Navier–Stokes:

  ∂_t u + (u·∇)u = −∇p + νΔu + f,  ∇·u = 0.

## Correspondence

| 𝓜 | Navier–Stokes |
|---|---|
| X | divergence-free velocity fields {u : ∇·u = 0} (or perturbations δ = u − ū about a mean flow) |
| Φ | the flow generated by F(u) = −(u·∇)u − ∇p + νΔu + f |
| K | incompressibility, boundary conditions, regularity conditions |
| B | kinetic energy B(u) = ½‖u‖²_{L²} |

## The balance decomposition is exact here

With suitable boundary conditions (no-slip or periodic), the convective term
is energy-neutral, ⟨(u·∇)u, u⟩ = 0 — pure **redistribution** across scales —
and the pressure term drops by incompressibility. The viscous term is pure
**dissipation**, ν⟨Δu, u⟩ = −ν‖∇u‖². Hence, for smooth solutions:

  d/dt ½‖u‖² = ⟨f, u⟩ − ν‖∇u‖²  —  *injection minus dissipation.*

In P-1's terms the energy functional sees β = 0: the nonlinearity adds no
energy, it only moves it between scales. Turbulence, in this frame, is a
constrained multi-scale propagation regime — complexity from redistribution
under injection–dissipation competition, not from energy creation. This
mapping is substantive, not a renaming: the decomposition is an identity, not
an analogy.

## Honest caveats

1. **Weak solutions.** In 3D the energy *identity* above is only known for
   smooth solutions; Leray–Hopf weak solutions satisfy an energy
   *inequality*. The direction still suffices for P-1-type bounds (R3).
2. **Regularity smuggling.** Placing "regularity conditions" inside K quietly
   assumes the hard part: 3D global regularity is open (a Millennium
   problem). K may *state* such conditions; the mapping must not pretend they
   are known to hold.
