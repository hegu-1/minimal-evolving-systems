## OP-16 reframe: the delta may be in the question class, not the theorem (Writer)

Maintainer-directed, following a question about how this project's starting
point differs from the literature it compares itself against.

**Observation.** OP-16's loci (i)–(iii) all ask "which theorem here beats a
classical one?" That is a framing inherited from the comparison targets, and
those targets are almost uniformly *modeling* work: a system is given, its
properties are derived. This project asks which objects are minimally necessary
for persistence and evolution — a *foundational* question class. The two produce
different things. Modeling naturally yields existence and characterization;
a foundational question naturally yields **impossibility and minimality**. The
latter are rare in modeling literature not because they are known but because
nobody has occasion to prove them.

This was not visible until P-10. Every earlier result here (P-1…P-9) was a
characterization result and every one of them came back classical — which read
as repeated failure. P-10 is the first impossibility result, and it is the first
whose closest prior work (arXiv:2603.19808) turns out to satisfy its hypothesis
rather than contain its conclusion. The pattern is worth naming: **the loci we
were searching may have been the wrong shape, not merely unlucky.**

**Guard, recorded with the reframe.** This is the most self-serving idea the
project has produced and must be treated accordingly. "We are not new because we
are foundational" would hollow out OP-16 completely. The reframe earns its place
only by producing specific statements the modeling literature would neither ask
nor answer — and those statements still face the ordinary literature search.
C-003 (v2) c₁ is the first candidate and its search is **incomplete** (abstracts
only; the Geritz et al. 1998 CS-citer check did not run). One instance is not a
pattern. If no second instance appears, the reframe is an excuse and OP-16
should have it deleted.

**Consequence for the roadmap, not yet acted on.** If the project's product is a
reformulation rather than a novelty theorem — a possibility already recorded in
C-003's own attack log — then the exit criterion in `ROADMAP.md` ("an external
mathematician reads `book/` and produces a substantive objection") is not a late
milestone but the *primary* one, and it is the only item on the list that no
additional theorem advances. It has never been attempted. P-10 is the most
suitable object to put in front of an outside reader: short statement, explicit
hypothesis, strong conclusion, and a named prior work to argue against.
